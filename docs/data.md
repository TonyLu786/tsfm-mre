# Data

TSFM-MRE reads daily U.S. Treasury constant-maturity yields from the Federal Reserve's
H.15 release through FRED, generates a synthetic panel calibrated to the same series, and
accepts any local CSV file of yields.

## H.15 Treasury yields (FRED)

| Series | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| `DGS3MO` | Market yield on U.S. Treasury securities at 3-month constant maturity |
| `DGS2` | Market yield on U.S. Treasury securities at 2-year constant maturity |
| `DGS10` | Market yield on U.S. Treasury securities at 10-year constant maturity |

The yields are quoted on an investment basis, in percent per year, daily, as published in
the Board of Governors' H.15 Selected Interest Rates release. Other constant-maturity
series (`DGS1MO`, `DGS6MO`, `DGS1`, `DGS3`, `DGS5`, `DGS7`, `DGS20`, `DGS30`) work the same
way.

**Retrieval.** `configs/fred_h15.yaml` downloads each series from FRED's public CSV
endpoint,

```text
https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=<SERIES>&cosd=<START>&coed=<END>
```

and stores the response unchanged in `data/raw/fred/` (relative to the working directory)
together with a JSON record of the
URL, the retrieval time and the SHA-256 digest. Later runs read the cache after checking
the file against its digest, and the run manifest lists every file used. The reference
results were computed from the following downloads (2003-01-01 to 2025-12-31):

**Calendar.** Bond-market holidays appear as missing values. The panel keeps the dates on
which all requested series are observed, 5,753 trading days for 2003–2025, so a horizon
of $h$ days always means the $h$-th next trading day. Observations are recorded in
hundredths of a percentage point; this resolution enters the randomised PIT.

## Synthetic H.15-calibrated panel

`configs/synthetic.yaml` runs the same experiment without network access on a synthetic
panel generated by `tsfm_mre.data.synthetic_panel`:

1. **Factor structure.** Yields follow the Nelson–Siegel (1987) representation with the
   Diebold–Li (2006) decay $\lambda = 0.0609$ (maturities in months),
   $y^*_t(\tau) = L_t + S_t \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda\tau}}{\lambda\tau} + C_t\left(\frac{1 - e^{-\lambda\tau}}{\lambda\tau} - e^{-\lambda\tau}\right)$.
2. **Factor dynamics.** Level, slope and curvature follow AR(1) processes with daily
   persistence 0.999, 0.9995 and 0.998 around means of 3.45, −1.75 and −1.0.
3. **Shocks.** Factor shocks share a GARCH(1,1) variance (Bollerslev, 1986) with
   $\alpha = 0.05$ and $\beta = 0.94$, have Student-t tails with 5 degrees of freedom, and
   are scaled by 2.5 relative to other days from 15 September 2008 to 31 March 2009 and
   from 2 March to 30 April 2020. Their covariance is set so that daily changes of the
   3-month, 2-year and 10-year yields match the H.15 volatilities and correlations over
   the sample.
4. **Zero floor.** Observed yields are $\log(1 + e^{k y^*}) / k$ with $k = 8$. The
   transformation is within one basis point of the latent yield $y^*$ whenever
   $y^* > 0.31\%$ and keeps short rates near zero in low-rate regimes.

## Local CSV files

With `data.source: csv`, `data.path` points to a file with a `date` column and one column
of yields (percent) per series. `data.resolution` sets the recording precision used by the
randomised PIT (0 for continuous data).
